# Docket Numbers — U.S. Power Reactor Units

Source: [NRC, *List of Power Reactor Units*](https://www.nrc.gov/facilities-safety/operating-reactors/list-power-reactor-units) (retrieved 2026-10-25). 95 operating units, in the NRC's alphabetical order.

A docket is the NRC's file number for a licensing action. Two forms appear in practice:

- **ADAMS / electronic form** — 8 digits, e.g. `05000397`. This is what the NRC's document system indexes and what
  appears in `ML...` accession packages and in citation tools.
- **Correspondence form** — e.g. `50-397`, used in letters, Federal Register notices and Technical Specifications.
  Convert by taking digits 2–3 as the part (`50` operating, `52` combined licence) and stripping leading zeros.

The first digit group also tells you the licensing framework: **050** = 10 CFR 50 operating licence (construction permit + operating licence, the older two-step process); **052** = 10 CFR 52 combined licence (early site permit + certified design + COL, used for new reactors such as Vogtle 3 & 4).

## 3. Using dockets with this corpus

```bash
# find the docket for a plant, then the NRC correspondence about it
grep -i 'columbia' DOCKET_NUMBERS.md
# docket 05000397 -> search ADAMS for the accession numbers
# https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/adams.html  (search by docket)
```

Caveats: the list covers **operating** units only (not shut-down or decommissioning plants,
not research/test reactors, not fuel-cycle facilities). Docket numbers never change, but licence
numbers, operators and power uprates do — re-check the NRC page for current ownership.
